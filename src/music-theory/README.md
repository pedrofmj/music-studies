# Music Theory And Keyboard Study

This collection is the written, systematic path for learning music itself. It
complements the device-specific material elsewhere in the repository: theory
provides the grammar, keyboard technique turns that grammar into movement, ear
work connects symbols to sound, and the instrument studies provide practical
applications.

Its place in the wider repository is tracked in the [Music Studies epic map](../../docs/music-studies-epics.md).

The intended learning cycle is:

~~~text
read and name the concept
  -> write or spell it without the keyboard
  -> play it slowly with deliberate fingering
  -> vary key, inversion, rhythm, and sound
  -> hear its musical function
  -> record the result and next problem
~~~

The repository contains notes and book recommendations, not copies of the
recommended books.

Follow these modules in order:

   scales, chords, inversions, arpeggios, and cadences into dependable keyboard

## Integrated Roadmap

The phases are sequential, but earlier work continues in small amounts after a
new phase begins. Use one main text at a time; the other books are references or
later specializations.

### Phase 0 - Prepare The Study System

- Choose one primary foundation text. The imported notes recommend *Alfred's
  Essentials of Music Theory* as the clearest starting point.
- Choose one keyboard course if basic reading and coordination are still new.
  *Alfred's Basic Adult All-in-One Piano Course* fills that role.
- Keep a notebook or dated Markdown log for written answers, fingerings,
  comfortable tempos, observations, and the next task.
- Use a metronome only after the notes and movement are understood slowly.

Done when the primary books, study location, keyboard, and first log entry are
ready.

### Phase 1 - Build The Language

Study notation, rhythm, intervals, major and minor scales, triads, seventh
chords, and basic harmonic function. For every new concept:

1. Name and write it.
2. Build it from intervals rather than memorizing only a shape.
3. Play it on the keyboard.
4. Hear it in a short progression or piece.

Start with a small set of keys, then expand systematically to all 12. Do not
wait for speed before learning what the notes mean.

Done when you can spell common scales and chords, locate them on the keyboard,
and explain the construction rather than only reproduce it by muscle memory.

### Phase 2 - Learn Keyboard Grammar

Use *The Complete Book of Scales, Chords, Arpeggios & Cadences* by Willard A.
Palmer, Morton Manus, and Amanda Vick Lethco as the primary technical reference.
Study one key as a connected system:

~~~text
scale
  -> diatonic triads
  -> chord inversions
  -> one-octave arpeggio
  -> expanded arpeggio
  -> cadence
  -> short progression or accompaniment
~~~

Add *Keyboard Harmony* when the basic shapes are stable so that chords,
voicings, and progressions become musical tools instead of isolated drills.

Done when each studied key can be explained, played with consistent fingering,
and used in a short musical context.

### Phase 3 - Train The Ear And Sound Judgment

Study one keyboard sound family at a time. Compare patches with the same scale,
arpeggio, chord progression, register, and dynamics so that the sound itself is
the changing variable. Record attack, sustain, body, brightness, texture,
musical role, and whether the sound works alone or in a layer.

Continue rhythm and ear work alongside this phase. The goal is to connect the
written concept, physical gesture, and heard result.

Done when you can identify the principal sound families, describe their useful
differences, and select sounds by musical function rather than by preset name.

### Phase 4 - Develop Harmonic Depth

After the foundations are reliable, continue with *Music Theory for Computer
Musicians* and *Keyboard Harmony*. Then choose the branch that matches the
musical goal:

- Jazz and extended harmony: *The Jazz Theory Book*, *The Jazz Piano Book*, or
  *Jazzology*.
- Classical harmony and voice leading: *Tonal Harmony*, *Harmony and Voice
  Leading*, or *The Complete Musician*.
- Broader piano technique and healthy movement: *The Complete Pianist*.

Advanced books are not a reason to abandon scales, arpeggios, rhythm, ear work,
or actual playing. They should explain and expand material that is already
being heard and applied.

## Default Session

For a 45-minute session, use this baseline and adjust it to the available time:

| Time | Work | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 minutes | Read, write, and name one concept | One written example or explanation |
| 15 minutes | Scale, chord, inversion, arpeggio, or cadence work | Clean repetitions at a recorded comfortable tempo |
| 10 minutes | Apply the material to a progression or short written piece | One musical use in the current key |
| 10 minutes | Ear, rhythm, or sound-family comparison | One concise listening observation |

Accuracy, evenness, relaxation, and understanding come before tempo. Stop and
diagnose tension or pain rather than practicing through it.

## Completion Standard

A topic is learned only when all four statements are true:

- I can explain or construct it without guessing.
- I can play it slowly and evenly with deliberate fingering.
- I can recognize or compare its sound in context.
- I can use it in a progression, accompaniment, improvisation, arrangement, or
  piece.
