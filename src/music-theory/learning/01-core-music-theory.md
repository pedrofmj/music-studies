

# 🎹 1. Core Music Theory (Start Here)

These build your *mental model of music*. Don’t skip this.

### 📘 *Alfred’s Essentials of Music Theory* – Andrew Surmani

- Very beginner-friendly but not shallow

- Covers notation, intervals, scales, chords, rhythm

- Good if you want something structured and progressive

👉 Best if you want a **clean starting point**

---

### 📘 *Music Theory for Computer Musicians* – Michael Hewitt

- Extremely clear explanations

- Very practical, not academic

- Great for understanding how theory applies in real music

👉 Best if you want **intuitive understanding quickly**

---

### 📘 *Tonal Harmony* – Kostka & Payne

- More “academic” and complete

- Used in universities

- Covers harmony, voice leading, analysis

👉 Best if you want **serious depth and rigor**

---

# 🎹 2. Keyboard-Focused Learning

Since you're starting with keyboard, these connect theory to the instrument.

### 📘 *Alfred’s Basic Adult All-in-One Piano Course*

- Combines technique + reading + theory

- Good if you're learning piano from scratch

---

### 📘 *The Complete Pianist* – Penelope Roskell

- Technique + musicality + injury prevention

- More advanced but very well-rounded

---

### 📘 *Keyboard Harmony* – Carl Humphries

- Teaches chords, voicings, progressions directly on piano

- Bridges theory → actual playing

👉 This one is **very important for practical application**

---

# 🎷 3. Jazz Theory (Highly Recommended for Depth)

Jazz theory will expand your understanding dramatically—even if you don’t plan to play jazz.

### 📘 *The Jazz Theory Book* – Mark Levine

- The **classic jazz theory book**

- Covers:
  
  - Chord-scale theory
  
  - Extensions (9ths, 11ths, 13ths)
  
  - Modal thinking

- Used by Berklee students

👉 Must-have if you're serious

---

### 📘 *The Jazz Piano Book* – Mark Levine

- Focused specifically on keyboard

- Teaches:
  
  - Voicings
  
  - Comping
  
  - Left-hand techniques

👉 Perfect companion to the above

---

### 📘 *Jazzology* – Robert Rawlins & Nor Eddine Bahha

- More structured and academic than Levine

- Great explanations of harmony and function

---

# 🎼 4. Harmony & Advanced Understanding

These take you beyond basic theory into real musical thinking.

### 📘 *Harmony and Voice Leading* – Aldwell & Schachter

- Deep dive into how chords actually move

- Essential if you want to compose or arrange

---

### 📘 *The Complete Musician* – Steven Laitz

- Combines theory, ear training, and analysis

- Very comprehensive

---

# 🎵 5. Rhythm, Ear, and Musicality

Theory alone won’t make you musical—these help connect it.

### 📘 *The Rhythm Book* – Richard Hoffman

- Deep understanding of rhythm (often neglected)

---

### 📘 *Functional Ear Trainer (book + app ecosystem)*

(Not purely a book, but worth mentioning)

- Helps you *hear* theory, not just understand it

---

# 🎯 Suggested Learning Path (Important)

Don’t try to read everything at once. Do this:

### Phase 1 (1–2 months)

- Alfred’s Essentials of Music Theory

- Alfred’s Piano Course

- - basic keyboard practice

### Phase 2 (2–4 months)

- Music Theory for Computer Musicians

- Keyboard Harmony

- Start playing simple songs

### Phase 3 (ongoing)

- Jazz Theory Book

- Jazz Piano Book

### Phase 4 (optional deep dive)

- Tonal Harmony

- Harmony and Voice Leading

---

# ⚠️ Key Advice (Most people get this wrong)

- Don’t just read → **play everything on the keyboard**

- Don’t memorize → **experiment**

- Learn chords in **all 12 keys early**

- Practice:
  
  - Major scales
  
  - Triads
  
  - 7th chords
